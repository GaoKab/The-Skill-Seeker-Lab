# EWAH Development Plan — From Imagery to Product

The concept phase is closed. No more looks get generated. This plan turns the strongest ideas into products that can be patterned, sampled, worn, washed, and sold.

---

## 1. The frozen first capsule — five hero products

**FROZEN.** Nothing enters or leaves this list during development. (This supersedes the earlier Phase 1 drop list in `05-phase-1-launch-plan.md`. The Curve Legging and long-sleeve Sculpt Tunic move to a later phase — their fit logic lives on inside these five.)

| # | Product | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Warrior Flow Dress-Skort** | Asymmetrical wrap front, mixed pleated and smooth panels, built-in shorts |
| 2 | **Summer Sculpt Tunic Dress** | The purple silhouette made sleeveless for summer — curved hem, sculpted seams, wearable alone or over shorts/leggings |
| 3 | **Center Pleat Training Dress** | Fitted sculpted bodice, partial pleated skirt, side opening, built-in shorts |
| 4 | **Sculpt Flow Set** | Structured short-sleeve top with loose technical pants |

Everything else on the boards — pleated flow dresses, the rust Warrior capsule, the long-sleeve tunics — waits until these five have working patterns.

## 2. The EWAH Design Standard — the filter

Locked rules. Every piece in development is scored against all ten; a design that does not follow **at least 8 of 10** (with rules 3, 9, and 10 always mandatory) does not belong in the first capsule — and this same filter gates every future design before it earns a tech pack.

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | Curved sculpting seams |
| 2 | Shape through the waist **without excessive compression** |
| 3 | Fuller-bust and fuller-hip accommodation *(mandatory)* |
| 4 | Matte, non-clinging performance fabric |
| 5 | Built-in coverage where needed |
| 6 | Pleats used selectively, not everywhere |
| 7 | Minimal front branding |
| 8 | Vertical logo or emblem at the back |
| 9 | **No front seams on leggings or shorts** *(mandatory)* |
| 10 | Coverage tested while squatting, bending and lifting *(mandatory)* |

## 3. The fit system — EWAH's real intellectual property

The logo is not the IP. **The fit system is.** Standard activewear placed on curvy models afterward would contradict the entire premise — these bodies are the starting blocks.

### The three launch fit bodies

| Fit body | Architecture | Primary conflicts to solve |
|---|---|---|
| **FB-1: Fuller hips & glutes, smaller waist** | High waist-to-hip differential, prominent glutes, fuller thighs | Waist gap; rear rise shortage; hem catching on the seat |
| **FB-2: Fuller stomach, substantial hips** | Front-waist fullness with real hip volume | Waistband roll/dig; front cling; front hem lifting |
| **FB-3: Fuller bust, shorter torso** | Bust ≥ 2 sizes above frame on a compressed vertical | Chest strain; shaping landing at the wrong height; tunic lengths overwhelming the frame |

### The nine specification dimensions — defined per garment, per fit body

Every garment spec sheet must state these values explicitly. "It stretches" is not a specification.

1. **Bust allowance** — ease over full bust, and how it's delivered (seam shaping vs. stretch zone)
2. **Waist-to-hip difference** — the differential the block is actually cut for
3. **Front and back rise** — separately; back rise always deeper on FB-1
4. **Garment length** — HPS-to-hem front and back, per fit body height assumption
5. **Rear coverage** — where the hem sits relative to the gluteal fold, standing *and* at squat depth
6. **Armhole depth** — curve bodies need mobility without side-bra exposure
7. **Sleeve circumference** — upper arm ease (a chronic standard-block failure)
8. **Built-in short inseam** — length + anti-ride construction
9. **Movement allowance** — added ease/stretch budget per movement zone (squat, hinge, overhead)

These specs get validated per fit body during sampling, then encoded into the graded blocks — the pattern library that becomes the protected asset.

## 4. Technical flats — the documentation standard

Generated imagery gives direction; a pattern maker cannot manufacture from it. Each of the five products requires a flats package containing:

- Front technical flat, back technical flat, side view where the silhouette demands it (wrap fronts, curved hems)
- Seam and pleat placement (every line drawn is a sewn line — no decorative ambiguity)
- Closures (type, length, position)
- Pocket locations and dimensions
- Hem shape with measurements
- Built-in short construction (attachment method, waistband, gusset, pocket)
- Logo dimensions and exact placement
- Fabric zoning (which fabric platform per panel — see `03-fabrics-and-materials.md`)
- Stitch type and finishing notes per seam

**Documentation order: Warrior Flow Dress-Skort first** — it contains most of EWAH's signature language. Its brief is `08-product-brief-warrior-flow-dresskort.md`.

## 5. Prototype order — one at a time

Do not prototype everything simultaneously. Each prototype answers questions the next one depends on.

### Founder testing round — budget: 2 looks

The first sampling round is funded for **two looks only**, made for the founder's personal training use (fit model zero, worn in real sessions daily). Chosen for maximum learning per unit of money and complete wardrobe coverage:

| Look | Product | Why it's in the round |
|---|---|---|
| **Look 1** | **Warrior Flow Dress-Skort** | Tests nearly the whole thesis in one garment: sculpted bodice, asymmetric wrap, pleat/smooth mix, built-in shorts + pocket, coverage in motion. Brief: `08` |
| **Look 2** | **Sculpt Flow Set** (top + flow pant) | Tests the separates construction family; fills non-dress training days; each piece also wear-tests against existing wardrobe items |

*Budget fallback:* the set is two patterns/two samples. If three garments total exceed budget, Look 2 becomes the **Summer Sculpt Tunic Dress** (one pattern, tests the fitted-tunic block + standalone modest wear).

*Deferred from this round:* Center Pleat Training Dress (reuses bodice lessons Look 1 will teach), Oversized T-Shirt Dress (cheapest to make, teaches least — simple jersey block, no curve engineering).

### Full prototype sequence (as funding allows)

| Order | Prototype | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | Warrior Flow Dress-Skort *(Look 1)* | The core proposition: sculpting, asymmetry, pleats, coverage and movement in one garment |
| **P2** | Sculpt Flow Set *(Look 2)* | Expansion into separates (top block + pant block) |
| **P3** | Summer Sculpt Tunic Dress | Curvy fit + modesty + standalone wear + warm-weather comfort |
| P4–P5 | Center Pleat Training Dress, Oversized T-Shirt Dress | Proceed **only after P1–P3 patterns are working** — they reuse the proven bodice, pleat, and coverage solutions |

## 6. Test the products like equipment

A dress looking beautiful while standing is not enough. Every sample goes through the full circuit, on fit-body-representative wearers:

**Movement:** deep squats · deadlifts · lunges · overhead presses · Smith-machine work · bench exercises · floor exercises · fast walking · sitting

**Durability & function:** repeated washing (minimum 5 cycles — pleats, branding, color) · sweat and opacity when wet · pocket security under impact · riding and rolling · **pleats snagging on gym equipment** (benches, pads, Smith rails, velcro belts)

Record results per garment per fit body. A failure is a pattern correction, not a footnote.

### The long throw-on dress is a separate safety category
The Oversized Training T-Shirt Dress works beautifully for warmups, walking, and some lifting — but excessive loose fabric around machines can be unsafe. **Market it honestly as a layer or controlled-movement garment, not as universally suitable for every gym activity.** Product page and hang tag state its intended use. EWAH's credibility rests on being truthful about what each garment is for.

## 7. Validation before manufacturing — the testing circle

Recruit **12–20 African and curvy women** across different sizes, faiths, ages, and gym experience. Show them the five pieces (samples in hand, not renders) and have them **rank and commit**, never just approve:

1. Which one would you buy **first**?
2. What would you actually wear it for?
3. Your preferred coverage level?
4. Your biggest current fit problem?
5. What price is acceptable — and at what price would you not buy?
6. Preferred dress length and short length?
7. Should built-in shorts be removable?
8. What would make you **return** it?

**Never ask "Do you like it?"** — people say yes to attractive ideas. Ask what they would purchase and what would stop them. The output is a ranked production order and honest price bands, not compliments.

## 8. Protect the concept selectively

The broad idea of modest activewear for curvy bodies cannot be meaningfully protected. Spend protection money only where it bites, and **not heavily before the fit and product are proven**:

| Priority | Asset | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Now (cheap) | Brand name + logo | Trademark registration in home market first |
| Now (free) | Technical drawings, design documentation, this repository | Dated, versioned records — authorship evidence |
| At sampling | Original patterns and graded blocks | Ownership clauses in every pattern-maker and factory agreement — EWAH owns the patterns, full stop |
| At production | Supplier and production agreements | NDAs + non-compete on the specific constructions |
| Later, if proven novel | Distinctive construction combinations (e.g., the dress-skort's wrap + pleat-zone + liner system) | Design-patent review — only after market proof |
