# Manufacturer Trust & IP Strategy — Protecting EWAH Before Launch

The founder's stated fear: **someone stealing the creation before launch.** Post-launch copying by other brands is accepted as inevitable; the requirement is manufacturers who are *faithful and truthful* during development. This document is the realistic threat model and the protection stack, researched July 2026.

---

## 1. The honest threat model — what actually happens

**Pre-launch theft of an unlaunched brand's designs by its own factory is rare — and it's rare for a structural reason:** a factory doesn't know which designs will sell. Copying costs them development effort and a client relationship for an unproven product. What factories actually copy is **proven sellers** — garments already moving units publicly (the Shein/fast-fashion pattern, which is the post-launch risk the founder has already accepted).

The pre-launch risks that *do* happen, ranked by likelihood:

| Risk | Likelihood | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Quality/delay/communication failure | **High — the real enemy** | Bad samples, missed dates, wrong fabric. This kills more brands than theft ever has |
| Pattern reuse | Moderate | Your developed block quietly offered to the factory's next client ("we have a nice dress-skort pattern...") |
| Overrun selling | Moderate (at production, not sampling) | Extra units of your run sold out the back door on marketplaces |
| Design showcased | Low-moderate | Your sample photographed into their catalog/showroom as "our capability" |
| True pre-launch design theft | **Low** | Factory launches your unlaunched design at scale before you |

**Coach's reframe:** guard against the moderate risks contractually (cheap, below), spend most energy on the high risk (vetting for competence), and don't let the low risk delay launch. Also: EWAH's deepest IP — the Curve Block geometry, the fit framework, the fit-body data — **never needs to be fully disclosed to any factory.** They receive garment-level specs; the system behind them stays home. A factory can copy a dress; it cannot copy the framework, the community, or the founder's fit data flywheel.

## 2. The protection stack (in order of cost-effectiveness)

### Tier 1 — Free, do now
1. **Information diet:** send factories only per-garment specs and measurements (`10` §2 already enforces this). Never the framework, the roadmap, or the full collection. Sample one capsule, not fourteen designs.
2. **Dated evidence trail:** this repository is a timestamped, versioned record of authorship — keep every design decision in it. Photograph/email-to-self all sketches and samples on receipt.
3. **Watermark** every board/image sent out: "EWAH — CONFIDENTIAL — property of [legal name]".
4. **Spread nothing before you must:** the RFQ needs reference images and one brief — quotes don't require tech packs. Full construction detail goes only to the ONE chosen factory, after the agreement is signed.

### Tier 2 — Cheap, do before sending tech packs
5. **Trademark** the EWAH wordmark + emblem in the home market first (already in `07` §8) — the brand name is the asset that matters most and is easiest to defend.
6. **Registered designs — the underused weapon.** For the 2–3 signature constructions (Dress-Skort, Sculpt Flow silhouettes): **UK registered design costs £50–150 for up to 50 designs and grants in ~3 weeks; EU is €350 for the first + €115 each for designs 2–10** in one multiple-design application. This is real, enforceable registered protection for the visual design at trivial cost — file from the technical flats before or at sampling. (Both systems also give a 12-month novelty grace period after first disclosure.) A **US design patent** is slower and costlier ($1.5–3k with counsel) — defer unless the US becomes the core market.
7. **The right contract for the region:**
   - **China: an NNN agreement, not an NDA.** A Western NDA is near-worthless there. An NNN (Non-disclosure, Non-**use**, Non-circumvention) must be governed by Chinese law, written in Chinese, with Chinese court jurisdiction and specific liquidated damages — then it's genuinely enforceable (foreign plaintiffs win the large majority of contested IP cases in Chinese IP courts). Cost via a China-focused firm: roughly $600–1,500 one-time, reusable across suppliers. Non-negotiable before tech packs go to any Chinese factory.
   - **EU/UK/Turkey/US factories:** standard NDA + development agreement with pattern-ownership clause works, backed by real courts.
   - **Everywhere:** pattern/block ownership clause — patterns developed from EWAH briefs are EWAH property, not to be reused for other clients (`09` §5 language).

### Tier 3 — Later, at scale
8. Split sourcing (bodice/skirt/assembly across factories) — real technique, overkill at 4 samples; revisit at 10k+ units.
9. Design-patent review for genuinely novel constructions — only after market proof (`07` §8 unchanged).

## 3. Region trade-offs for a trust-first small brand

| Region | Advantages | Disadvantages | Trust/IP picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **China (Guangdong/Fujian)** | Deepest performance-fabric supply chain; best price; pleating/liner expertise everywhere; MOQ 50–300 findable | Distance; communication load; IP weakest *without* NNN; inspection needed | Fine **with** NNN + escrow payments + customs-record vetting (`11` §2). Certifications (BSCI/WRAP/OEKO-TEX) = reputational skin in the game |
| **Vietnam** | Excellent technical performance wear; rising quality reputation | Less small-brand friendly (MOQs often 500+); fewer startup-oriented houses | Moderate; same contract logic as China |
| **Turkey (Istanbul)** | Strong knitwear; EU-adjacent shipping; certifications standard among exporters; mid pricing; MOQs ~100 findable | Lira volatility; fewer activewear-dress specialists | Good — closer legal reach, export culture built on EU client trust |
| **Portugal** | Premium knit quality reputation; boutique factories at MOQ 100–300; **EU courts = strongest IP enforceability**; easy factory visits | Highest unit cost of the offshore options; capacity often booked | **Best trust profile per euro** — strong candidate for the founder's fear profile |
| **Mauritius / Morocco / SA / Kenya** | Duty advantages; brand-story alignment; Mauritius/Morocco have real knit capability | Fewer candidates; pleating capability must be verified carefully | Moderate-good; smaller industry = reputations are checkable |
| **US / local** | Maximum trust + legal reach; fastest iteration; visitable | 2–4× unit cost; MOQ small but sampling $$ | Highest trust, highest cost — good for P1 fit iteration even if production goes offshore |

**Strategy that fits the fear:** run the RFQ across **two regions** — one Chinese specialist (cost + technical anchor, NNN in place) and one Portugal/Turkey boutique (trust anchor). Compare samples. The trust premium of Europe is often only a few dollars per unit at EWAH's price point ($60–90 retail) — cheap insurance if the fear would otherwise slow you down.

## 4. What "faithful and truthful" actually looks like (and how to cause it)

You cannot buy loyalty with a contract — contracts set the downside; **incentives create faithfulness.** A factory is faithful to clients who are worth keeping:

- **Clear specs, consolidated feedback, prompt payment** — the `10` §6 discipline makes you a dream client; dream clients get protected
- **A growth story told honestly:** "sampling now, 100–300/style on fit approval, reorders on sell-through" — factories invest in clients with a future
- **Verification over vibes:** customs records (ImportYeti), certifications, production photos, escrow payment — per `11` §2. A factory that accepts verification is signaling honesty; one that resists is answering your real question
- **Relationship behaviors that matter:** video calls with the actual merchandiser (not just sales), remembering names, small consistency tests (do they follow up when they said?), visiting when possible
- Red flags recap: name-dropping without proof, instant quotes with no fit questions, resistance to written terms, bare-wire-transfer-only payment

## 5. On coaching programs and "gurus"

The founder's instinct is right. Most paid brand-building programs resell three things: a supplier list (replaceable by `11` + ImportYeti), template documents (this repo already exceeds them), and accountability (free with discipline). The seller's income is usually the program, not the brand. **Nobody with a genuinely profitable secret sells it for $997.** Where outside help IS worth paying for, pay specialists per-deliverable, not gurus per-dream: a China lawyer for the NNN (~$1k), a technical designer for flats (~$300/style), a QC inspection firm per shipment (~$300), an IP attorney hour for the design filings (~$300). Total: less than most courses, all of it real.
