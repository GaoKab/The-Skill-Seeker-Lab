# PULA — specialty print partner round 1

**Purpose:** find a printer who can physically produce the PULA tee as designed — puff print plus reflective — rather than describe it. This is the print side of `26` and `27`. The garment itself (blank sourcing, 250 gsm tee) sits with ASBX in `24`.

**Why this round exists:** the artwork in `pula-artwork/v2/` separates puff from reflective as isolated plates precisely so a printer can quote each one. Until one of them says yes to both techniques, PULA has artwork and no way to print it.

## The non-negotiable technical ask

Two specialty passes on one garment:

1. **Puff / HD raised print** on the headline type.
2. **Reflective** on the rain elements (7 of the 75 drops in v2 are the reflective plate).

A printer who offers only standard plastisol or standard embroidery cannot make this garment. That is the screening question and it is worth asking first, because it eliminates most shops in one email.

### WizardPins — different product, open thread

This thread is the enamel pin and raindrop keyring, not the tee. Pricing is already on the record from 14 Sept (hard enamel 1", pin + card set: $14.28 at 25, $7.28 at 50, $4.38 at 100, $3.54 at 250, $2.60 at 500; 1.25" and 1.5" keychain bands quoted alongside), plus a standing 10% offer. They also confirmed double-sided keychains can carry different artwork per side **as long as both sides share the same outer shape and cut line** — which is a real constraint on the raindrop silhouette.

On 30 Sept the position taken was the correct one: not ready for a bulk order, but yes to the free mockup. They replied the same day asking for the design file.

**This is a decision, not a task.** Sending artwork to get a free mockup means handing a vendor the raindrop silhouette with no agreement in place, which cuts against the information diet that governs every other thread in this project. Two defensible answers:

- **Send a deliberately reduced file.** The raindrop outline alone, no type, no anthem line, no colour system. Enough for them to mock up a shape and quote a size; not enough to be the object. The pin is a silhouette anyway, so this costs almost nothing in usefulness.
- **Hold until there is a reason to order.** Objects are not on the critical path. The tee and the headwear are. A mockup that cannot be acted on for months is a vanity artefact.

Either is fine. What is not fine is sending the full artwork package to an unsigned vendor because they asked nicely.

## What round 1 settles

- The screening question works and should lead every approach: *do you print puff and reflective on the same garment, in-house?*
- One of five is eliminated, one address is dead, two are too young to judge, one is a different product line.
- **Three live print leads is not enough.** Rodriguez's answer suggests the specialty-print population is thinner than assumed, so round 2 should go out wider rather than waiting on three.

## Round 2 — before chasing round 1

Build the list first, send once. Target shops that advertise puff or 3D print and reflective ink as named services, not general-purpose promo printers. Minnesota and Chicago local for the ability to inspect a physical sample by hand, plus any specialty shop nationally that names both techniques.

The ask stays the same length and shape as round 1, and still carries no artwork.
