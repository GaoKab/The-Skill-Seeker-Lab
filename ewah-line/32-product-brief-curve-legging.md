# Product Development Brief — EWAH Curve Legging (EWAH-CL-07)

**Round 1, substituting for ST-02.** Adopted 27 Sept 2026. Companion to `08` (WF-01), same authority and same form.

**Why this replaced the summer dress.** ST-02 is described in `17` as the "short breathable summer hero." Sampling it in October means testing a summer garment through a Minnesota winter and launching it in spring at the earliest. The Curve Legging is season-correct, is the highest-repeat category in activewear, and is the most unforgiving test of both the block and the factory. ST-02 and its mesh-platform test move to Round 2, which defers rather than removes the mesh risk that four of eight capsule styles depend on.

**Budget position unchanged.** Round 1 remains two garments: WF-01 + CL-07.

---

## 1. Customer problem

A woman with a 52 cm difference between waist and hip buys leggings that fit her thighs and seat, then spends the session pulling the waistband up. The band gapes at the small of the back, folds over when she bends, and rolls down through squats. She owns several pairs and none of them stay where she put them.

This is not a preference and not a sizing mistake. It is **Failure 1** in `06`: linear grading from a straight block, which assumes a waist-to-hip ratio around 0.78–0.80 and produces a waistband drawn for a body that does not exist at her hip measurement.

**Founder's measured case, and it is more extreme than the framework's own assumption:**

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Bust | 104 cm |
| Waist, smallest | 80 cm |
| Waist, at navel | 86 cm |
| Hip, fullest | 132 cm |
| **W:H, true waist** | **0.61** |
| Hip minus waist | 52 cm |

A mainstream chart places a 132 cm hip in a band whose waist is drawn at roughly 110–115 cm. **The legging that fits her hips has a waistband about 30 cm too big.** That gap is the entire product.

## 2. The ratio problem this brief has to solve

`06` Failure 1 specifies the Curve Block at **W:H ≈ 0.70–0.75**. `02` repeats it as 0.72–0.75. **The founder is 0.61, outside EWAH's own framework.**

This is a real decision and the brief resolves it as follows.

**Cut the Round 1 sample at the founder's actual measurements, 0.61.** She is the only fit tester available, she is the fit authority, and a sample built to a hypothetical 0.72 customer fails on the only body that can judge it.

**Do not let 0.61 silently become the production block.** A range built solely on it would fail a 0.72 customer nearly as badly as the market currently fails the founder, in the opposite direction. After the sample passes, the grading decision in `09` has to span roughly **0.61 to 0.78**, with the founder marked as the deep end rather than the centre. That is a two-block decision with real cost, and it belongs after a proven sample, not before.

**Practical instruction to the factory:** grade this sample to the supplied measurements. Do not apply your standard waist-to-hip grading rule. The ratio is deliberate and it is the product.

## 3. Intended activities

Weight training, squats and hinge movements, floor work, treadmill and stair machine, stretching, and the walk to and from the gym in cold weather. Worn under a tunic or dress as often as alone, so the waistband sits under other garments without bulk.

## 4. Silhouette

Full length, high rise, second-skin through calf and ankle, sculpted rather than compressed flat. The leg line should read long and clean; the seams should look deliberate, not orthopaedic. **7/8 length as a second option once the full length passes** — not sampled in Round 1.

## 5. Waistband engineering — the piece that decides everything

This is where the garment succeeds or fails. **Failures 1 and 4.**

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| Height | **10 cm**, measured at centre front |
| Back rise | **Higher at centre back than centre front.** The band follows the body's natural waist angle rather than sitting level. This is the single most important line in the brief |
| Shape | **Contoured, conical, not tubular.** Cut as a shaped panel narrowing toward the waist, not a straight rectangle joined into a tube |
| Structure | Power-knit lining. **No rope elastic, no drawcord, no exposed elastic band** |
| Front rise | **No front rise seam.** Diamond gusset construction |
| Finish | Flat, no visible topstitch ridge that prints through a fitted top |

**The factory question that matters:** ask whether they cut the waistband as a shaped panel or as a straight strip. A straight strip on a 52 cm waist-to-hip difference will gape no matter how well it is sewn, and a factory that only does straight strips cannot make this garment.

## 6. Seam map

- **Outer thigh sweep** — a long contour seam from hip through to knee, shaping the outer line
- **Back seat lift** — a V-back yoke seam that lifts and separates the glutes rather than flattening them
- **No inner-thigh seam through the crotch.** Diamond gusset only
- Flatlock or coverstitch throughout. No overlock seams against the skin at the waist or inner thigh

Seams are structural here, not decorative. Each one is doing shaping work the fabric cannot do alone.

## 7. Fabric

**Platform A, 220–240 gsm** (`03`): 73–78% recycled polyamide / 22–27% elastane, warp-knit or circular interlock, matte buttery hand.

**Non-negotiable: squat-proof at maximum zone stretch.** Failure 5 exists because fabric is normally tested flat rather than at the stretch a 132 cm hip actually creates. Test on the body, in bright light, at full depth. Every colourway.

**Colourway for Round 1: Onyx only.** Black is the hardest colour to keep opaque under stretch and the one every customer buys first. If it passes in Onyx it passes.

## 8. Fit specifications for the sample

Cut to the founder's measurements in section 1. Full length to ankle. Additional measurements required before cutting, to be supplied with the flat measurements of the reference garment:

- Waist to floor, outside leg
- Waist to hip at fullest, vertical
- Thigh at fullest
- Knee
- Calf at fullest
- Ankle
- Inseam

## 9. Branding

Gold EWAH mark, small, at centre back waistband exterior. Nothing on the leg. Nothing on the front.

## 10. Testing criteria — pass or fail for prototype approval

Run the `06` Part 3 protocol. The legging passes only if **all** of these hold:

1. **Tug count zero** across a 45-minute session. Every adjustment is a logged failure with a cause
2. **No waistband gape** at the small of the back, standing or seated, measured by sliding a flat hand behind the band
3. **No roll-down** through ten full-depth squats
4. **No fold-over** when seated and bending forward
5. **Fully opaque** at maximum stretch, full squat, bright overhead light
6. **No front seam definition.** Gusset works
7. **Recovery** at the knee and waist after a full session with no bagging
8. **Sits flat under a fitted tunic** with no band ridge printing through

Failure of 1, 2 or 5 is fatal and requires a new pattern rather than an alteration.

## 11. What Prototype 1 must answer

1. Can this factory cut a contoured, back-higher waistband from supplied measurements, or does it default to a straight band?
2. Does Platform A at 220–240 gsm hold opacity at 0.61 proportions in black?
3. Does the diamond gusset eliminate front seam definition at this hip-to-waist difference?
4. Is the V-back yoke doing visible shaping work, or is it decoration?
5. What does the factory's own grading rule assume, and how far did it have to depart from it?

## 12. Questions to send the factory with this brief

Send these alongside the brief once the agreement is signed. They are commercial and technical, not design, so they can go early.

1. Do you cut leggings waistbands as shaped contoured panels, or as straight strips joined into a tube?
2. Can you grade to a supplied waist-to-hip ratio that departs significantly from your standard rule, and what is your standard rule?
3. What is your sample cost and lead time for one full-length compression legging on a new block?
4. What compression knit do you hold or source at 220–240 gsm with 22–27% elastane, and can you send a fabric swatch before sampling?
5. Do you test opacity at stretch, and will you photograph the squat test on a form before shipping?
6. Diamond gusset construction, standard for you or not?
