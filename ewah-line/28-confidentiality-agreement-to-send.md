# The agreement EWAH sends to suppliers (NNN, one page)

**Why this file exists.** Two suppliers have now said the same thing: the buyer provides the agreement. Kira at Hongyu, 4 Sept: *"The NDA will need to be provided by you, the buyer, then we can sign it."* ASBX wants the tech pack and has not offered any agreement at all. Aung Crown was the exception, not the rule. Until this document exists, both threads sit still and no design pack can leave.

**What this is.** A short mutual Non-Disclosure, Non-Use and Non-Circumvention agreement. Not a Western NDA, which only stops disclosure. The three N's matter because the realistic risk is not a factory publishing the tech pack, it is a factory **using** the block for its own line or **selling around** the brand to its customers. Structure follows the Aung Crown template, which is a sound model and was already reviewed in `14`.

**What this is not.** Legal advice, and not a substitute for a lawyer once there is money at stake. It is the zero-budget instrument from `14` §7, written to be good enough to send today rather than perfect in six months. The clause that actually earns its keep is 2(b), the ownership of patterns and blocks. That is the one to never delete for any supplier's convenience.

## Send-ready text

> **MUTUAL NON-DISCLOSURE, NON-USE AND NON-CIRCUMVENTION AGREEMENT**
>
> This Agreement is made on ______________ between:
>
> **Party A (Disclosing Party):** Gaofenngwe Kabubi, trading as EWAH, of ____________________________
>
> **Party B (Receiving Party):** ____________________________, of ____________________________
>
> The parties intend to discuss the development and manufacture of garments and accessories (the "Project"). In the course of those discussions Party A will disclose confidential information to Party B, and Party B may disclose confidential information to Party A. Each party wishes to protect its information.
>
> **1. Confidential Information.** "Confidential Information" means any information disclosed by one party to the other in connection with the Project, in any form, whether or not marked confidential, including designs, drawings, technical packs, patterns, blocks, specifications, measurements, fit data, artwork, samples, prototypes, materials and trim sources, pricing, customer and supplier information, and business plans.
>
> **2. Obligations of the receiving party.** The receiving party shall:
>
> (a) keep the Confidential Information strictly confidential, disclose it only to those of its employees and subcontractors who need it for the Project and who are bound by equivalent obligations, and not disclose it to any other person without the disclosing party's prior written consent;
>
> (b) use the Confidential Information only for the Project. **Any patterns, blocks, graded size sets, samples, prototypes, technical files, artwork and moulds developed from Party A's Confidential Information are and remain the exclusive property of Party A, and shall not be used for, shown to, adapted for, or disclosed to any other client, nor used in the receiving party's own products, catalogue, samples, marketing, showroom or trade show displays;**
>
> (c) not apply for or register any intellectual property right derived from the disclosing party's Confidential Information, including any patent, design, trademark or copyright registration;
>
> (d) not circumvent the disclosing party by dealing directly with any customer, supplier, agent or contact introduced by the disclosing party, in a manner that excludes the disclosing party from the Project or from any transaction arising out of it;
>
> (e) on written request, return or permanently delete all Confidential Information and all copies of it within ten (10) business days, and confirm in writing that it has done so. This obligation does not require the destruction of records the receiving party must keep by law.
>
> **3. Exclusions.** Clause 2 does not apply to information which the receiving party can prove by written record: (a) it already lawfully held before disclosure; (b) is or becomes public through no act or omission of the receiving party; or (c) was lawfully received from a third party without restriction.
>
> **4. No licence, no obligation.** Nothing in this Agreement grants any licence or right in the Confidential Information beyond the Project, or obliges either party to enter into any further agreement.
>
> **5. Duration.** The obligations in clause 2 continue for five (5) years from the date of this Agreement. The ownership provision in clause 2(b) continues without time limit.
>
> **6. Remedies.** The parties agree that damages alone may not be an adequate remedy for a breach of this Agreement, and that the disclosing party is entitled to seek injunctive relief in addition to any other remedy.
>
> **7. Governing law and disputes.** This Agreement is governed by the laws of ____________________. Disputes shall be resolved by the courts of ____________________, or by arbitration at ____________________ if the parties agree.
>
> **8. Entire agreement.** This document is the entire agreement between the parties on this subject and may only be amended in writing signed by both parties. It may be signed in counterparts, and a scanned or electronic signature is as valid as an original.
>
> **Party A**  Name: ____________  Signature: ____________  Date: __________
>
> **Party B**  Company: ____________  Name and title: ____________  Signature / company chop: ____________  Date: __________

## Filling in clause 7 before sending

This is the only clause that changes per supplier, and getting it wrong makes the rest decorative.

| Supplier | Put in clause 7 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Hongyu (China)** | Laws of the People's Republic of China; courts of the supplier's own city, or CIETAC arbitration | A PRC-law agreement enforceable in a Chinese court is worth something. A UK- or US-law NDA against a Chinese factory is close to worthless in practice, because enforcing a foreign judgment there is slow and usually not attempted |
| **ASBX (Portugal)** | Laws of Portugal; courts of Braga or Porto | EU jurisdiction, genuinely enforceable, no special drafting needed |
| **Aung Crown (China)** | Not applicable | They provided their own, already reviewed. Do not send this one on top of it |

**Signature discipline, same as with Aung Crown.** Party B signs with the **registered legal entity name**, not a trading or website name, and Chinese suppliers apply the **company chop**, not only a signature. A Chinese contract without a chop is weak. Ask for the entity's business licence number on the signature block if it is not already there.

Party A signs as **"Gaofenngwe Kabubi, trading as EWAH"**, since EWAH is not a registered company yet. That is honest and enforceable. It also means the IP sits with the founder personally, which is correct at this stage and can be assigned into a company later.

## What this does not protect against

Worth saying plainly rather than pretending otherwise. This document raises the cost of copying and gives a real basis to complain, and most factories will simply comply because the business is worth more than the block. It will not stop a determined bad actor, and litigating across borders is beyond this budget. The practical protections remain the ones in `14`: split the work so no single supplier holds everything, keep the emblem hardware separate from the garment maker, pay through escrow, and move quickly enough that being copied matters less than being first.
