# Product Development Brief — EWAH Warrior Flow Wrap Dress (EWAH-WW-08)

**Round 2 hero.** Adopted 27 Sept 2026. Same authority and form as `08` (WF-01) and `32` (CL-07).

**Runs only after Round 1 passes its decision gates.** The sequencing discipline in `17` is not suspended for this piece.

**Source material:** collection architecture `01` §04, board `boards/warrior-flow-wrap-dress-board.webp`, and the campaign boards filed 27 Sept.

---

## 1. Why this is the Round 2 hero, and the reason is structural

Every other garment in the line must be graded precisely to a waist-to-hip ratio. **A wrap closure with a tie accommodates a range of ratios by construction.** The wearer sets the waist herself, every time she puts it on.

That matters more for EWAH than for most brands. The founder measures **0.61**, the fit framework assumes **0.70–0.75**, and the market assumes **0.78–0.80**. The wrap is the one silhouette that serves that whole span without three separate blocks. It is the lowest-risk way to reach the widest part of the intended customer, and it happens to also be the most distinctive thing the brand makes.

The second reason is coverage. Long sleeve, maxi length, high wrap neckline, worn over a legging. **This is the piece that makes the campaign imagery true** — including the hijabi model who appears in both campaign boards while the roadmap still files hijab-compatible dressing under "future."

## 2. Customer problem

A woman who trains covered has two bad options. Loose modest clothing that hides her shape and catches on equipment, or performance wear that fits but exposes. She ends up layering a gym kit under a garment never designed to move, then managing both for the whole session.

**Failure 8** in `06`: modest clothing makes women feel hidden rather than powerful, because coverage is achieved by adding shapeless volume. **Failure 3**: long garments cling to the stomach and seat instead of covering them. **Failure 6**: garments ride up because hem geometry ignores motion arcs.

This dress answers all three with structure instead of fabric.

## 3. Intended activities

Strength training, mobility and stretching, floor work, walking and treadmill. **Not** running or high-intensity intervals — the maxi length and open slits are wrong for that and the brief should not pretend otherwise. It is also explicitly a gym-to-street piece: it has to survive the walk in, the session, and the walk out without looking like activewear.

## 4. Silhouette

Long-sleeve wrap dress, maxi length, worn over a legging or the built-in short alone. Contoured through the bodice, released from the waist into a full skirt with **dual side slits**. Cinched at the waist by an **obi-style wrap belt** — the African-Asian fusion signature made structural rather than decorative.

Reference colourway **Rust**. Onyx and Aubergine follow.

## 5. The obi belt — the piece that decides the garment

This is where the design lives, and it is also the biggest risk. A tie belt that slips, bunches or has to be re-tied mid-session fails the tug-count test on its own.

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| Width | Wide obi form, **12–15 cm at centre front**, tapering toward the ties |
| Construction | Self-fabric, interlined so it holds its width and does not rope or twist |
| Wrap | Long enough to pass around the body **once and tie at the front or side**, with the tie ends reaching roughly mid-thigh |
| Anchoring | **Belt loops or an internal stay at the side seams** so the belt cannot ride up over the ribs or slide down over the hip |
| Under-closure | The wrap bodice closes with **an internal tie or snap before the belt goes on.** The belt must be decorative-structural, never the only thing holding the dress closed |

**The internal closure is non-negotiable.** A wrap dress whose modesty depends entirely on an external belt will open during a session, and for a garment built on covered training that is not a fit failure, it is a product failure.

## 6. Seam map

- **Contoured princess seams** through the bodice, front and back, carrying bust shaping (Failure 2)
- **Waist seam** at the natural waist, where the skirt is released
- **Vertical spine seam** at centre back carrying the tone-on-tone EWAH branding
- Skirt panels shaped so the fullness falls from the waist seam and **hangs off the seat rather than catching on it** (Failure 3, engineered float)

## 7. Slits and coverage

- **Dual side slits**, rising to roughly **mid-thigh**, positioned on the side seam
- Slit height is a validation question, not a fixed number. Proto 1 at mid-thigh; the testing circle decides whether it rises or drops
- **The built-in short must make the slit safe at any height.** Coverage comes from the liner, not from restraint in the slit

## 8. Built-in short

Follow the liner specification in `08` §6 exactly — attached liner, no front rise seam, diamond gusset, flat coverstitched inseams, 13 cm inseam base, right-side drop-in phone pocket, no silicone gripper, liner opacity squat-proof independently of the skirt.

**One addition specific to this garment:** because the dress is maxi length with open slits, the liner is visible more often than in WF-01. It must read as **designed coverage in the same colourway**, never as underwear glimpsed through a gap.

## 9. Fabric

**Platform D, 200–230 gsm** (`03`): modal-poly-elastane or heavy matte poly jersey. Matte, fluid, with real drape weight.

**Drape is the performance requirement here, not stretch.** The skirt has to fall and swing rather than cling, and a fabric that is too light will wrap around the legs during movement while one that is too stiff will read as costume. Ask the factory for **three drape weights in the 190–240 gsm range** and choose on a hanger, not a swatch card.

The bodice and sleeves may need a firmer hand than the skirt. **Ask whether a two-fabric construction is feasible** — Platform A compression knit through the bodice, Platform D drape jersey from the waist down. If it adds meaningful cost, single-fabric D is acceptable for Proto 1.

## 10. Branding

Tone-on-tone vertical EWAH spine branding at centre back, per the placement board. Nothing on the front. The obi belt carries no branding.

## 11. Testing criteria — pass or fail

Run the `06` Part 3 protocol, plus these:

1. **Tug count zero** over 45 minutes, and the belt is counted — a single re-tie is a failure
2. **The wrap does not open** during overhead reach, forward hinge, or floor-to-stand
3. **The belt does not migrate** up over the ribs or down over the hip
4. **Skirt clears the seat** when walking and does not catch on it
5. **Slits do not expose** beyond the liner at full stride or in a lunge
6. **Liner is opaque** at squat depth, bright light, in every colourway
7. **Sleeves do not ride** at the cuff during overhead work
8. **Nothing catches** on a bench, a rack, or a machine. Walk the garment round a real gym before approving it
9. **Reads as clothing, not activewear**, walking down a street

Failures 1, 2 and 8 are fatal and require a pattern change rather than an alteration.

## 12. What Prototype 1 must answer

1. Can the belt be anchored so it holds position without restricting the ribcage?
2. Does the internal closure hold the wrap independently of the belt?
3. Does Platform D at this weight drape and swing, or cling?
4. Is a two-fabric bodice-and-skirt construction worth the cost?
5. At what slit height does coverage stop depending on the wearer thinking about it?
6. Does the garment clear gym equipment, tested in an actual gym?

## 13. Questions to send the factory with this brief

1. Have you built wrap garments with an anchored obi or sash belt, and how do you stop the belt migrating?
2. Can you construct one garment from two fabric platforms, compression knit bodice into drape jersey skirt, and what does that add per piece?
3. What matte drape jerseys do you hold or source between 190 and 240 gsm, and can you send three weights as hangers rather than swatch cards?
4. Sample cost and lead time for a long-sleeve maxi wrap dress with built-in liner short?
5. How do you finish a slit edge on a drape jersey so it does not curl after washing?
