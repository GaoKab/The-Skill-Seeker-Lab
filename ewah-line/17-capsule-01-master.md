## What the master pack adds that the repo now treats as binding

- **Per-style factory questions** — include them in the RFQ; they do the vetting for you
- **Development deliverables ladder** (swatches → proto + measurement report → written change log → sealed size set → inspected bulk) — this is the factory contract skeleton
- **Fit validation bodies A/B/C** + at least one petite-curvy and one tall-curvy wear test before final grading — matches framework `06`; now explicitly a gate
- **Legal/commercial terms** (pattern ownership after payment, no reuse/display of rejected samples, no substitutions without written approval, sealed-sample rule, documented defect/late-delivery remedies) — fold into the development agreement
- **Founder decision gates** — the five questions asked of every sample, ending with the best one: *would it still be recognizable as EWAH without a visible logo?*

## Notes held over from the previous plan (still true)

1. **Budget reality (`14` §7):** Round 1 = two samples ≈ **$150–400 all-in**. If cash allows only one, WF-01 goes first alone — it remains the capability test.
2. **Mesh caution:** Capsule 01 leans heavily on perforation/mesh (4 of 8 styles). That's a new fabric platform with real risks the pack itself acknowledges (snag, edge curl, shade-matching mesh to solid). Round 1's ST-02 is the cheap test of this platform before Rounds 2 and 4 depend on it — if ST-02's mesh disappoints, Rounds 2/4 re-sequence rather than push through.
3. **Zero-budget protections** unchanged: information diet (send WF-01 + ST-02 briefs only — never the full 8-product pack to a factory that hasn't signed terms), watermarks, escrow payments, evidence trail.
4. **RFQ flow (`10`)** unchanged, now carrying WF-01 + ST-02 with their per-style factory questions.

## Open design decision for WF-01 (before flats)

Two skirt-geometry variants exist on the boards (`01` catalogue, `boards/wf01-variant-*.jpeg`): **tulip wrap** (soft curved overlap) vs **angular wrap** (pointed asymmetric hem, longer back). Proto 1 commits to one; the other becomes a later derivative. Trade-off: tulip drapes safer over fuller hips; angular is more distinctly EWAH-architectural but its hem points need motion validation.
