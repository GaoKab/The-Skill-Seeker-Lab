# Starting samples now, and the storefront decision

## Correction: the measurement gate was overstated

This log has said for twelve days that "measurements block all samples." That is true for **grading a size range**. It is not true for **getting the first sample cut**, and treating them as the same thing has cost nearly two weeks.

What each thing actually needs:

| To do this | You actually need | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Hat sample | **One number: head circumference** | 30 seconds |
| First dress sample | Flat measurements of a garment that already fits, plus bust, waist, hip | 20 minutes |
| Grade a full size range | The 13-number set in `09` §6, on three fit bodies | A proper session, later |

The first sample is never the final fit. That is what the remote fit loop in `10` §6 exists for. A factory needs enough to cut something real and close; it does not need the complete anthropometric picture before it starts.

### The reference garment route, which is faster and better

There is a black cotton V-neck tee dress already in the wardrobe, the one worn to the gym on 2 September. Factories generally prefer a reference garment to a measurement list, because a garment removes ambiguity about how measurements were taken.

Flat-measure it on a table, garment laid flat, no stretching:

| Point | How to measure |
|---|---|
| Chest width | Armhole to armhole, 2.5cm below the armhole seam, then double it |
| Waist width | Narrowest point, doubled |
| Hip width | Widest point below the waist, doubled |
| Total length | High point of shoulder straight down to hem |
| Shoulder to waist | High shoulder point to the narrowest point |
| Armhole depth | Shoulder seam down to the underarm point |
| Neck width and drop | Seam to seam, and centre front depth |
| Hem sweep | Across the opening, doubled |

Then say what to change about it, in plain language. Longer or shorter. Closer or looser through the hip. Higher neckline. Built-in short at this length. That is a workable first-sample brief, and it carries the founder's actual body knowledge rather than a number set typed from a tape measure held at slightly the wrong angle.

Three body numbers alongside it, taken over close-fitting clothes: **bust at fullest, waist at narrowest, hip at fullest.** That is the curve ratio the whole brand rests on, and it is the number that tells a factory this is not a standard block.

## The storefront

### Do not build one storefront for two brands

EWAH and PULA were declared separate in writing to a supplier. They have different customers, different price points, different production routes and different stories. Putting them on one site to save a subscription undoes that, and it is very hard to unpick later once the SEO, the email list and the customer records are mixed.

Two storefronts. They do not have to cost twice as much, because they do not launch at the same time.

### PULA needs checkout in about ten days. EWAH does not need one for months.

That difference decides everything about the sequencing.

| Brand | What it needs now | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **PULA** | A real checkout by roughly 25 September | The BOIPUSO 60 date is 30 September and there is product to sell |
| **EWAH** | An email capture page, nothing more | There is no product. A store with nothing to buy converts nobody and drains money monthly |

### Platform recommendation at zero budget

For PULA's two or three tee designs, a full Shopify subscription is the wrong first spend. Verify current pricing before committing, as these plans change:

| Option | Cost | Fits PULA? |
|---|---|---|
| **Big Cartel** | Free tier, up to 5 products | **Yes, and this is the recommendation.** Built for exactly this: a handful of products, an artist or small label, no monthly fee |
| **Payhip** | Free, ~5% per transaction | Yes. Unlimited products, you pay only when you sell, which is the right shape when volume is unknown |
| **Square Online** | Free tier, card processing only | Yes, and strongest if any of this sells in person at an event |
| **Shopify** | Monthly subscription, promotional first month | Later. Better once there is real volume, more than five products, and the monthly fee is paid for by sales rather than hope |

**Recommendation: Big Cartel free tier for PULA now, migrate to Shopify when monthly sales cover the subscription several times over.** Moving later is a morning's work at this size. Paying a subscription for months before launch is money you said you do not have.

For **EWAH**, a single page with an email field. Carrd, a free Shopify-adjacent landing page, or one static page on the eventual domain. What matters is that it captures the addresses of the women who will test the first samples, because those twelve to twenty fit testers are the launch list.

### The thing to sort before any platform

**Payments, and where the customers are.** PULA's buyers are Botswana and the diaspora. The founder is in the United States. That creates two real problems no storefront solves:

1. **Shipping cost.** A tee from the US to Botswana can cost more than the tee. If a meaningful share of buyers are in Botswana, printing and fulfilling there beats shipping from America, even at a worse unit price.
2. **Currency and card acceptance.** Check that the chosen platform's payment processor accepts the cards the intended buyers actually hold.

Decide who the PULA customer physically is before choosing where it is printed. A diaspora-first collection sold in dollars and shipped domestically in the US is a completely different operation from a Botswana-first collection, and the second one probably should not be printed in America at all.

### Domains

Two, cheap, bought once. Check availability and register them before announcing anything, because a name that cannot be had is not a name.

## The clock that has not moved

**14 days to 30 September.** The PULA vectors are still not commissioned. The honest arithmetic: vectors 2–3 days, DTF sample 2 days, production run 5–7 days, plus shipping. That fits, barely, and only if the artwork starts immediately. Every day it waits, the finish degrades from screen printing with puff and reflective to plain DTF, and then the date itself goes.

A storefront with nothing to put in it is not the bottleneck. The artwork is.
