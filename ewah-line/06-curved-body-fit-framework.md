# The EWAH Curved Body Fit Framework

> **Your body was never the sizing problem. The clothes were.**

This is the document that makes EWAH fundamentally different from ordinary activewear. It is built **before** more garments are drawn, because every future style inherits from it. Ordinary activewear starts from a garment sketched on a narrow body standard and stretches it into larger sizes. EWAH starts from the bodies — their architectures, their fit failures, their movement needs, their coverage standards — and engineers garments outward from there.

**The three principles every decision runs through:**

1. **Built for curves** — the garment blocks, proportions, and seam placements are developed around curvy body architectures. Not inclusive sizing bolted onto a straight block.
2. **Engineered coverage** — coverage must hold during squatting, bending, lifting, running, and floor work. Modesty is functional in motion, not merely visual while standing still.
3. **Sculpted, not exposed** — the clothes celebrate shape without tracing or exaggerating every part of the body.

---

## Part 1 — Body architectures (fit profiles)

Standard sizing captures circumference. It does not capture **proportion** — a woman can be curvy at a size 6, 10, 16, or 24. EWAH's long-term design system is therefore organized around fit profiles, not size labels.

| Profile | Defining architecture | Where standard blocks fail |
|---|---|---|
| **Fuller hip & glute** | Pronounced waist-to-hip differential; prominent glutes; fuller thighs | Waist gap when hips fit; rear rise too short; back waistband pulls down in squat; thigh fits but knee/calf bags |
| **Fuller bust** | Bust ≥ 2 sizes larger than frame; fullness forward and high | Front hem lifts; strain lines across chest; necklines gape or strangle; "size up" ruins shoulder and waist fit |
| **Fuller stomach** | Fullness at front waist/high hip; often paired with slimmer legs | Waistbands roll or fold under; fitted tops cling and ride at the front; leggings dig and create a shelf |
| **Curved hourglass** | Balanced fuller bust + small waist + fuller hip | Nothing fits the waist without straining bust and hip; loose enough for curves = shapeless at the waist |
| **Petite curve** | Any curve profile on a shorter frame / shorter torso | Rises too long; waist shaping lands at the wrong height; tunic lengths become dresses; sleeves and hems swamp |
| **Tall curve** | Any curve profile on a longer frame / longer torso | Rises too short; tops become crops in motion; shaping lands too high; inseams end awkwardly |

**Rollout is phased — this is a design system, not a launch requirement:**

- **Phase 1:** one **EWAH Curve Block** engineered around the fuller hip & glute + curved hourglass architectures (the largest shared failure set — the waist-versus-curves conflict). Graded XS–3XL on curve-adjusted rules.
- **Phase 2:** length variants of the same block — **Petite Curve** and **Tall Curve** (cheap to add: same shapes, adjusted torso/rise/hem lengths).
- **Phase 3:** **Fuller Bust** and **Fuller Stomach** block variants on proven styles (adjusted front length, dart-equivalent seam distribution, waistband engineering).

Every product page eventually states which profiles a garment is engineered for — fit honesty as a brand feature.

## Part 2 — Fit failure catalogue → engineering responses

The eight failures curvier women live with, each mapped to its real cause and EWAH's block-level answer. This table is the brief for every pattern maker and factory EWAH works with.

| # | Fit failure | Root cause in ordinary activewear | EWAH engineering response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Leggings fit thighs, gap at waist | Linear grading assumes a straight-block waist:hip ratio (~0.78–0.80) | Curve Block cut at W:H ≈ 0.70–0.75; contoured (conical, not tubular) back waistband; graduated internal elastic; higher back rise |
| 2 | Tops fit waist, strain at bust | No bust accommodation in stretch-knit blocks — fabric stretch is asked to do a dart's job | Dart-equivalent shaping built into sculpted seam lines; front panel length added; stretch allocated by zone, not uniformly |
| 3 | Longer tops cling to stomach and seat instead of covering | Straight-hanging excess fabric follows the body it touches | **Engineered float**: semi-fitted through ribs, then a structured A-flare released from the waist seam; fabric weight/drape chosen so the hem hangs off the body's widest point instead of catching on it |
| 4 | Waistbands roll, fold, or cut in | Thin elastic fighting the body's shape; band height uniform front and back | Wide (8–10 cm) contoured band, higher at back; power-knit lining, no rope elastic; band angle follows the body's natural waist curve |
| 5 | Transparency across fuller areas | Fabric tested flat, not at the stretch levels fuller hips/glutes/thighs actually create | Squat-proof test performed **at maximum zone stretch on curve-proportioned bodies**, every colorway; minimum opacity spec written into the fabric platform |
| 6 | Garments ride up during movement | Hem geometry ignores the body's motion arcs; fabric grips at the widest point | Curved shirttail hems, side slits placed for stride; hem circumference sized to clear (not grip) the seat; drape weight anchors the hem |
| 7 | Plus sizes are shapeless, basic, or visually apologetic | Larger sizes are simplified to save cost; design codes dropped | Every size carries the full design language — sculpted seams, pleats, gold branding, hidden performance. No "simplified" extended sizes, ever |
| 8 | Modest clothing makes women feel hidden, not powerful | Coverage achieved by adding shapeless volume | Sculpted seam language shapes silhouette *through* structure; coverage and beauty engineered together — sculpted, not exposed |

## Part 3 — Movement & coverage standards

Coverage that only works standing still is decoration. Every EWAH garment must pass this protocol **on curve-proportioned bodies across the size range** before approval — worn in a real training session, never assessed on a hanger or a straight-size fit model.

| Test | Movement | Pass criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **Squat test** | 10 full-depth squats | No transparency at seat/thigh; waistband stays anchored (≤ 2 cm migration); top hem keeps seat covered at depth; no drag lines exposing shape |
| **Hinge test** | Deadlift setup, toe touches | Back waistband gap ≤ 1 cm (no gape, no exposure); top back hem stays below seat; neckline doesn't fall open |
| **Floor test** | Plank, glute bridge, mat rolls | Nothing rides, pools, or twists; shorts liner stays put; band doesn't roll under front fullness |
| **Overhead test** | Press, hang, reach | Top front hem rises ≤ 5 cm and **returns on its own** — no tugging down between reps |
| **Run/impact test** | 5-minute run or skipping | No ride-up creep, no bounce-driven migration, no chafe lines; phone pocket holds without slap |
| **Heat test** | Full session sweat | Wicking works; no dark clinging patches at chest/back/seat; vent zones don't become see-through when wet |

**The tug count** — the single most honest fit metric: how many times the wearer adjusts the garment in a 45-minute session. **Target: zero.** Every tug is a fit failure with an addressable cause in Part 2.

## Part 4 — Measurement system & fit panel

- **Measure proportion, not just size.** Beyond standard girths, capture: waist:hip ratio, front vs. back waist height, rise depth seated, torso length HPS–waist, bust prominence (chest-over-bust differential), thigh at widest, seat depth.
- **The founder is fit model zero.** The line exists because you train — your gym wardrobe's best and worst pieces are the first data: measure what you love wearing and document *why* it works.
- **Build a fit panel of 6–10 African women** spanning the profiles in Part 1 and the size range ends (not just size M): recruit from your own gym. Every sample round is worn by the panel through the Part 3 protocol; feedback recorded per failure category, not as vague "it feels nice."
- **Grade within the block, not across bodies.** Curve-adjusted grade rules (larger hip increments, adjusted rise, controlled waist growth) so a 3XL is the same *architecture* as a S — never a stretched drawing.

## Part 5 — What this framework demands of the founding pair

The strongest first products are the two that solve the waist-versus-curves conflict together.

### EWAH Sculpt Tunic — the hero
Not a generic long shirt: a technical training top engineered to —

1. **Accommodate a fuller bust without pulling** — seam-built bust shaping, zoned stretch (Failure 2)
2. **Define the waist gently** — sculpted seams shape without compression (Principle 3)
3. **Float over the stomach** — engineered float construction (Failure 3)
4. **Cover the rear without gripping it** — hem circumference clears the seat; drape anchors (Failures 3, 6)
5. **Stay down during lifting and squatting** — passes squat, hinge, and overhead tests tug-free (Part 3)
6. **Vent heat without turning transparent** — opacity-specified vent zones, wet-tested (Failure 5, heat test)
7. **Work on shorter and taller women** — designed for Petite/Tall length variants from day one (Part 1, Phase 2)
8. **Look beautiful outside the gym** — full design language: sculpted seams, curved hem, gold branding (Failure 7)

### EWAH Curve Legging — the partner
One job stated plainly: **eliminate waist gaping while fitting fuller hips and thighs.** Curve Block W:H ratio, contoured high back rise, wide anchored waistband, zone-tested opacity, glute-contouring seam map that shapes without tracing (Failures 1, 4, 5).

Together they are the proof of the brand: *the pair that finally fits.* Every later garment — the dresses, the pleats, the Warrior Flow pieces — inherits its blocks, its tests, and its honesty from this framework.
