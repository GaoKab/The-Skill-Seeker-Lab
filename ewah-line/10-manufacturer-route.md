# Manufacturer Route — Getting the Founder Round Made by a Factory

**This is the operative sourcing plan.** The three looks (Warrior Flow Dress-Skort, Sculpt Flow Set, Throw-On Layer Dress) go to an activewear manufacturer for sample development, with the same factory positioned to run production when the fit is proven. The local pattern-maker pack (`09`) remains the fallback if factory sampling stalls.

## Why this route, and its one real trade-off

**For:** factories have in-house pattern makers, performance fabrics on the shelf, pleating capability, and liner-short experience; sample fees are often lower than Western freelance rates (typically **$50–150 per garment**, often credited against a future bulk order); and the pattern developed is already in the factory's system — no translation step between development and production.

**Trade-off:** no live fittings. Each fit round = ship, wear-test, document, revise = **2–4 weeks per cycle including courier**. Manage it by getting the first sample as close as possible (send exact measurements + a reference garment) and by documenting fit feedback like an engineer (photos, video, measured deviations) — never "it feels tight."

## 1. Where to find the manufacturer

| Channel | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Alibaba** (verified suppliers) | Search "activewear tennis dress built-in shorts manufacturer", "yoga dress OEM small MOQ", "pleated skort manufacturer". Filter: Verified Supplier + Trade Assurance, 5+ years, activewear as main line. Guangdong (Guangzhou/Dongguan), Fujian (Xiamen), Zhejiang are the clusters |
| **Sewport / Maker's Row / FashionGo sourcing** | Platforms matching brands to factories incl. small-batch; Maker's Row for US-based |
| **Turkey (Istanbul) / Portugal** | Strong knitwear, EU-adjacent lead times, MOQs sometimes 100/style; good if shipping/duties to your market favor them |
| **African route** (Mauritius, Morocco, South Africa, Kenya) | Brand-story + duty advantages per `03`; Mauritius and Morocco have real performance-knit capability — vet pleating specifically |
| Referrals | Ask other small activewear brand founders (Instagram DMs work) who makes their samples — the single highest-quality channel |

Shortlist **5–8**, RFQ to the best **3**.

## 2. The RFQ package (what you send)

1. **Intro + intent** (template §3) — sample development now, production intent at 100–300 pcs/style if fit succeeds. Factories prioritize clients with production intent; say it plainly and honestly
2. **Per-garment spec**: the `08` brief for the Dress-Skort; one-page specs for the Set and Layer Dress (silhouette description + reference board image + key measurements + fabric platform from `03`)
3. **Reference images**: `boards/summer-line-01.png`, `boards/wrap-dresskort-reference-onyx.png`, `boards/warrior-flow-dress-hero.png`, `boards/throw-on-layer-dress.jpeg`
4. **Your full measurement set** (`09` §6) with instruction: *draft the sample to these measurements, not your standard size chart* — this is non-negotiable and is also your first vetting filter
5. **Fabric requirements** by platform (A / B / D from `03`) — ask what they stock that matches; request swatches with the quote

Do **not** send: the full repo, business plans, the fit framework text, or anything beyond what's needed to quote and cut.

## 4. Vetting — the five filters

1. **Do they accept custom-measurement sampling?** If they insist on their standard size chart, they've failed the brand's core requirement — walk away
2. **Show me built-in-short dresses and heat-set pleats you've made.** Real photos, not catalog renders
3. **Fabric transparency:** will they send swatches + composition/gsm specs before sampling? (Guessing fabric by photo is how samples die)
4. **Communication quality in the first 3 emails** — slow, vague, or ignores your questions now = worse under deadline
5. **Sampling terms in writing:** cost per style, revision cost, lead time, sample fee credited against bulk (standard — ask)

Red flags: quotes instantly without asking a single fit question; "no problem" to everything; requires bulk commitment before first sample; won't do Trade Assurance/escrow-style payment.

## 5. Cost & timeline picture (3 looks / 4 garments)

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Sample round 1 (4 garments × $50–150) | $200–600 |
| Courier (samples to you, per round) | $40–80 |
| Revision round (usually 50–100% of sample fee, fewer garments) | $100–300 |
| Fabric swatches | usually free / token |
| **Total to a proven fit** | **~$350–1,000** |
| Sampling lead time per round | 2–3 weeks + shipping |
| Realistic total to final approved samples | **8–14 weeks** |

Payment: sample fees via Trade Assurance / PayPal / platform escrow — never bare wire transfer to a first-time partner.

## 6. The remote fit loop (replaces live fittings)

1. Sample arrives → run the `08` §10 test circuit in real training sessions for 1–2 weeks
2. Document every failure **measurably**: photo/video at squat depth, exact cm of ride-up, where the waist gaps and by how much, pin-and-photograph corrections on the body
3. Send one consolidated revision document per garment (numbered points, photos, target measurements) — never dribble feedback across messages
4. Repeat once. If a garment isn't right after two factory revisions, that garment goes to the `09` local route to fix the block, then the corrected pattern goes back to the factory

## 7. Protecting the work in this route

- Sampling agreement or written email confirmation: designs and specs are yours, patterns developed from your briefs are not to be sold or reused for other clients
- Watermark board images sent out ("EWAH — confidential")
- Realism: offshore sampling always carries some copying risk; your protection is speed, brand, and the fit system (which lives in your documents and community, not in any single garment a factory can copy)
