# EWAH Production Roadmap

From boards → wearable samples → sellable stock. Realistic timeline: **6–9 months** from first factory contact to first delivery.

## Stage 0 — Lock the foundation (Weeks 1–3)
- [ ] Finalize Phase 1 style list (see `05-phase-1-launch-plan.md`) — resist sampling everything
- [ ] Turn `02-tech-specs.md` into full tech packs for Phase 1 styles only (flat sketch front/back, measurement chart size M, BOM, construction notes). A freelance activewear tech-designer can do this in 1–2 weeks (~$150–400/style on Upwork/fashion networks)
- [ ] Define the **fit block**: since the brand promise is "engineered for African and curvier bodies, not resized," decide the base size and measure real bodies (yourself + 3–5 women across the size range). Your own gym wardrobe = the fit reference — measure the pieces you already love wearing
- [ ] Register the EWAH trademark (wordmark + emblem) in your home market before factory outreach

## Stage 1 — Factory sourcing (Weeks 2–6, overlaps Stage 0)
- [ ] Shortlist 5–8 full-package activewear factories with: dresses-with-built-in-shorts experience, in-house or partner pleating, low MOQ (100–300 pcs/style)
- [ ] Send tech packs to top 3 for quotes; compare FOB price, sample cost, MOQ, lead time
- [ ] Order fabric swatch cards + lab dips (Onyx, Aubergine, Bone first)
- **Red flags:** no performance-wear references, won't share client photos, quotes without asking fit questions

## Stage 2 — Sampling (Weeks 6–16)
Budget **2–3 sample rounds per style**; pleated + built-in-short styles rarely pass on round 1.

| Round | Purpose | Cost guide |
|---|---|---|
| Proto 1 | Prove silhouette + construction (any close fabric/color) | $80–200/style |
| Proto 2 | Correct fabric, correct color, fit corrections | $80–200/style |
| Pre-production (PP) sample | Exact bulk fabric, trims, branding — the contract reference | Often free/credited against order |

**Fit-test every round in an actual workout:** squats, floor work, running, overhead. Check: shorts riding, pleat behavior in motion, slit modesty mid-lunge, phone pocket bounce, sheerness when stretched, wicking. This is the brand's whole edge — never approve a sample off a hanger.

- [ ] Grade approved styles across XS–3XL with **curve-adjusted grade rules** (bigger hip increments, adjusted rise) — not standard linear grading
- [ ] Order a graded size set (or at least XS / M / XL / 3XL) and fit-test on real bodies at each point

## Stage 3 — Pre-production (Weeks 14–18)
- [ ] Approve PP samples in writing, photograph everything
- [ ] Confirm: bulk fabric lab-dip approval, branding placement/durability after wash test, care labels + legal compliance for target market, packaging (recycled polybag + hang tag)
- [ ] Negotiate terms: typical 30–50% deposit, balance before shipment; target 45–60 day production lead time
- [ ] Book a third-party inspection (AQL 2.5) for the first order — ~$150–300, worth it

## Stage 4 — Production + delivery (Weeks 18–30)
- [ ] Bulk production
- [ ] Mid-production check-in (ask for photos/videos off the line)
- [ ] Final inspection → ship (air for first small drop ~5–8 days; sea 30–45 days but far cheaper)
- [ ] Import duties/VAT in your landed-cost math from day 1

## Money model (per-unit, indicative)

| Item | Warrior Flow Dress | T-Shirt Dress | Curve Legging |
|---|---|---|---|
| FOB (factory, at 150–300 pcs) | $14–22 | $7–11 | $9–14 |
| Freight + duty (landed) | +20–35% | +20–35% | +20–35% |
| Landed cost | ~$18–28 | ~$9–14 | ~$12–18 |
| Retail (3.0–3.5× landed, premium-modest positioning) | $60–90 | $30–45 | $40–60 |

**Phase 1 cash estimate** (4 styles × 2 colors × ~150 pcs avg = ~1,200 units): tech packs + sampling ~$2.5–5k, bulk ~$14–22k, freight/duty/inspection ~$4–7k, branding/packaging ~$1–2k → **~$22–36k** all-in. Halve it by launching 3 styles in 1–2 colors each.

## Principles
1. **Small first order, fast reorder** — sell out at 150 units and reorder in 45 days; never sit on 1,000 units of an unproven style
2. **You are the fit model and the first customer** — the line exists because you train in it; every approval decision runs through "would I wear this for a real session?"
3. **One hero, photographed relentlessly:** the Warrior Flow Dress is the brand-defining piece
4. **Lock fabric platforms before adding styles** — new styles on proven fabric are cheap; new fabrics are expensive
