# Pattern Maker / Sample Maker Commission Pack — Founder Testing Round

> **Route note:** the operative sourcing plan is the **manufacturer route** (`10-manufacturer-route.md`) — factory sample development with production intent. This local-maker pack is the **fallback** (if factory sampling stalls or a block needs hands-on correction, §6 of doc `10`) — and its measurement sheet (§6) and screening logic apply to both routes.

Everything needed to commission the funded looks locally: who to look for, what to send, the outreach message, the scope of work, the agreement terms, and how to compare quotes. Goal: samples on your body, iterated through real training, without factory minimums or factory lead times.

## 1. Who you're looking for

One person or a small studio combining **pattern making + sample sewing**, with:

- **Stretch/activewear experience** — knits, elastane, flatlock/coverstitch equipment (a coverstitch machine is near-essential; ask directly)
- Willingness to do **iterative fittings on a live model (you)** — 2–3 fittings per garment, not a one-shot service
- Comfort signing a simple **work-for-hire agreement** (EWAH owns the patterns)
- Bonus: experience drafting for curvier blocks, or openness to your measurements as the base block instead of a standard size chart

Where to find them: local fashion-college alumni boards and technician networks, bridal/alterations studios that also draft (many have the skills), Instagram local sewing studios, and freelance platforms (Upwork/Etsy "custom activewear pattern") if local search runs dry — local is strongly preferred for fittings.

## 2. What you send them (the commission package)

1. This scope of work (§4)
2. Product brief `08-product-brief-warrior-flow-dresskort.md` (Look 1)
3. Reference images: `boards/wrap-dresskort-reference-onyx.png`, `boards/summer-line-01.png`, `boards/warrior-flow-dress-hero.png`
4. The Design Standard and fit-system pages (`07-development-plan.md` §2–3)
5. Your measurement sheet (§6 below — taken before first contact)
6. The agreement (§5)

Do **not** send the whole repository or business plans — briefs and references only.

## 3. Outreach message (template)

> Hello [name],
>
> I'm developing the first prototypes for EWAH, a performance-wear brand engineered for African and curvier bodies. I'm looking for a pattern maker/sample maker with stretch and activewear experience for a paid two-garment prototype round, with fittings on me as the fit model.
>
> The work: (1) a sleeveless training dress with a wrap front, mixed pleated and smooth skirt panels, and an attached liner short — I have a full written brief with measurements, seam maps, and construction notes; (2) a two-piece set: structured short-sleeve top and loose tapered training pants; (3) a simple long sleeveless layer dress with soft drape and side slits. Fabrics are performance knits (nylon-elastane and a pleat-holding poly) plus a drape jersey; I can supply fabric or we can source together.
>
> I'd like: first pattern + toile/first sample, two fitting revisions per garment, and final corrected patterns delivered to me in cut-ready form (paper or digital). I'd ask you to sign a short agreement confirming the patterns and designs remain my property.
>
> Could you tell me your availability, your rate for this kind of round, and a little about your activewear/knit experience? Happy to send the full brief.
>
> [Your name]

## 4. Scope of work — three looks, four garments

| Item | Look 1: Warrior Flow Dress-Skort | Look 2: Sculpt Flow Set (top + pant) | Look 3: Throw-On Layer Dress |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pattern drafting | From brief `08`, on founder's measurements as base block | Top: sculpted seams, fluted short sleeve. Pant: loose taper, sculpted seams, elastic cuff, side pockets, wide waistband | Sleeveless long layer dress, soft drape, small side slits, no liner (layers over own shorts/leggings) — simplest pattern in the round |
| Samples | Sample 1 → fitting → corrections → Sample 2 → fitting → final tweaks | Same cycle per piece | One sample + one correction round is usually enough |
| Fittings | Minimum 2 per garment, on the founder, including movement checks (squat, hinge, reach — from `08` §10) | Same | 1–2 fittings; drape + length + slit height checks |
| Deliverables | Final corrected pattern (all pieces, notched, labeled, seam allowances stated), final sample garment, construction notes | Same | Same |
| Fabric | Supplied by EWAH (~2.5 m platform A + ~1 m platform B per dress; ~3.5 m total for the set) | Same | ~2.5 m soft drape jersey (platform D or C weight) |
| Not in scope | Grading, tech packs, production — this round proves fit only | — | — |

**Budget frame (adjust to local market):** custom pattern + 2-sample development typically runs **$150–450 per garment** with an independent maker (dress-skort at the top of the range due to pleats + liner; layer dress at the very bottom, ~$100–200 with its single revision). Total round estimate **$550–1,300** plus fabric (~$80–200). If quotes come in high, drop Look 3 first — it's an accessory to the test, not the test. Get two or three quotes; the cheapest is not the goal — the one who asks fit questions is.

## 5. Agreement terms (one page, plain language)

1. **Work for hire / ownership:** all patterns, drafts, toiles, and derived documents created under this engagement are the exclusive property of [founder name / EWAH]; the maker retains no rights to reuse, sell, or reproduce them
2. **Confidentiality:** briefs, designs, and business information stay private; no posting samples or drawings without written permission (portfolio use may be granted later, by agreement)
3. **Deliverables + schedule:** as §4, with dates per milestone
4. **Payment:** per milestone — e.g. 40% on start, 30% at first fitting, 30% on delivery of final patterns and samples. Never 100% upfront
5. **Revisions:** two fitting revisions per garment included; further rounds at an agreed hourly/flat rate
6. Physical + digital pattern files handed over at completion — **EWAH keeps the masters**

## 6. Before first contact: the measurement sheet

Have these taken (by the maker at first fitting is fine, but self-taken starts the conversation): height · full bust · underbust · natural waist · high hip · full hip (widest) · thigh (widest) · knee · torso length HPS–waist · waist–knee · front rise & back rise (seated comfortable) · upper arm · armhole-depth comfort check. Record in cm. Note which existing garments in your wardrobe fit best/worst at waist vs. hip — bring both to the first fitting; they say more than numbers.

## 7. Comparing candidates — ask all three the same five questions

1. "What's your experience with elastane knits and liner shorts?" *(listen for coverstitch/flatlock talk)*
2. "How would you handle a 0.72 waist-to-hip ratio block?" *(anyone who says "just take in the waist" doesn't understand the project)*
3. "Heat-set pleats on poly — can you do them or source them locally?" *(pleating services exist in most garment districts; the maker doesn't need the machine, just the plan)*
4. "How many fittings do you recommend for a first block?" *(the right answer is 2–3, not "one should do it")*
5. "Are you comfortable signing pattern-ownership terms?" *(a professional says yes without hesitation)*

## 8. After delivery — the test loop

Final samples enter the `08` §10 test circuit immediately: wear each in real sessions for two weeks, log every tug and failure against the fit-failure catalogue, wash five times. What survives becomes the graded-block candidate; what fails goes back as a paid revision or a pattern note for round two. Either way the patterns — EWAH's real IP — are now physical, owned, and improving.
