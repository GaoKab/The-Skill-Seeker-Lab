## 2. The "we produce for Alo / Skims" claim — how to treat it

Someone claiming they manufacture for Alo, Skims, and top brands is making the **single most common sales pitch in apparel sourcing** — sometimes true, usually not, and occasionally true-but-irrelevant. Facts:

- Alo's documented exclusive producers include MAS Active (Sri Lanka) and KLW/Puku (Vietnam); Skims works with partners like Eurotex (Turkey) plus China/Thailand facilities. These are **large contract manufacturers under strict NDAs** — a genuine tier-1 supplier to those brands typically *cannot legally name them* in a sales pitch, and their MOQs (10k+ units) would exclude a startup anyway.
- What the claim usually means, at best: they're a **subcontractor of a subcontractor**, they bought similar fabric, or they copied the styles. At worst it's pure invention.
- **The claim itself is a yellow flag, not a credential.** A factory that leads with dropped names instead of asking about your specs is selling, not engineering.

### Verify in 15 minutes — customs records, not promises

1. **ImportYeti** (importyeti.com, free): search the factory's legal name as *shipper*. Real US-bound export history shows actual consignees, dates, and product descriptions. Claimed brand missing from years of records = claim dismissed. (Panjiva/ImportGenius are the paid equivalents.)
2. Ask for the **factory's legal registered name + address**, then check it — a company that resists giving its legal name is done.
3. Ask: *"Show me production photos of garments like mine — dresses with built-in liners, heat-set pleats."* Real factories show real work (with labels blurred); name-droppers show catalogs.
4. **Certifications beat name-drops:** BSCI/WRAP (ethics), OEKO-TEX (chemical safety), GRS (recycled content) are auditable third-party proof.

**Bottom line:** don't chase the lady's claim — test her factory like any other candidate: same RFQ, same custom-measurement requirement, same sample. If her sample beats the others on stitch quality and fit, she earns the order on merit. "Produces for Skims" is worth exactly nothing next to a sample in your hands that survives ten squats and five washes.

## 3. What "top quality" objectively means (put these in the RFQ)

| Marker | Standard to demand |
|---|---|
| Fabric | Composition + gsm certified per platform (`03`); OEKO-TEX; lab dips approved before bulk |
| Stitching | Flatlock/coverstitch on body seams; 12–14 SPI; bartacks at stress points; no skipped stitches on stretch test |
| Stretch & recovery | Waistband recovers after 30 wears; no seam pop at full stretch |
| Opacity | Squat-proof at max stretch, wet and dry, every colorway |
| Pleats | Heat-set permanent; survive 5+ washes |
| Consistency | Pre-production sample = bulk (AQL 2.5 inspection written into the order) |
| Symmetry & finish | Even hems, aligned seams, clean zip garages, no puckering — check the *inside* of the sample first; quality factories are proudest of the inside |

A $60–90 retail garment is built or lost on these, not on which celebrity brand the factory claims.
