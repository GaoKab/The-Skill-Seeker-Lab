# Botswana Independence Day Fundraising Plan

**Goal:** Raise funds for transportation and lodging for our group's Independence Day trip.
**Deadline:** 30 September (Boipuso) — 2026 marks **60 years of independence**, a powerful hook for donors and sponsors.
**Time available:** ~3 weeks (started 4 September).

---

## Step 1 — Set the target (Day 1–2)

You cannot fundraise effectively without a number. Fill this in first:

| Item | Details | Est. cost (BWP) |
|---|---|---|
| Transport (bus/kombi hire or fuel) | ___ people, ___ km round trip | |
| Lodging | ___ people × ___ nights × rate | |
| Meals / incidentals | | |
| Buffer (10–15%) | | |
| **TOTAL TARGET** | | **P ______** |

Divide the total by the number of group members — that's your "per head" figure. Sponsors respond well to concrete asks: *"P450 covers one person's transport and two nights' lodging."*

## Step 2 — Set up how money comes in (Day 1–3)

Open your channels **before** you start asking, and keep them few and traceable:

- **Mobile money:** Orange Money, Mascom MyZaka, or BTC Smega — one dedicated number, controlled by the treasurer.
- **Bank account / FNB eWallet** for larger sponsor payments (use the group's account, not a personal one, if possible).
- **Cash:** issue a simple numbered receipt for every cash donation.
- **Diaspora/international supporters:** a trusted contact abroad with PayPal/Wise who transfers the total in one go (note: major crowdfunding sites like GoFundMe generally don't support withdrawals to Botswana, so a diaspora relay is usually the practical route).

Appoint **one treasurer + one checker**. Post a running total publicly (WhatsApp group / notice board) every 2–3 days — transparency drives more giving.

## Step 3 — The three-week timeline

### Week 1 (Sept 4–10): Set up and start the pledge drive
- Finalize the budget and target; agree roles (coordinator, treasurer, publicity, events).
- **Launch a pledge drive immediately** — this is your fastest money. Every member asks 5–10 family members, friends, and colleagues for a fixed pledge (e.g. P50/P100/P200) via mobile money. A 20-person group each raising P300 in pledges = P6,000 in week one.
- Write and send **sponsorship letters** (template below) to 10–15 local businesses: transport companies, lodges, general dealers, hardware stores, salons, network operators' local dealers. Ask early — businesses need a week or two to decide. Ask for in-kind support too (a bus, discounted rooms) — a lodge donating two rooms is as good as cash.
- Create a simple flyer/poster (phone-designed is fine) with the 60-years theme, your target, and payment numbers. Share on WhatsApp statuses, Facebook community groups, and church/kgotla announcements.
- **Open the PULA / Rain Objects pre-order** (see Step 4) — printer quotes and the order form must be live this week for delivery before the 30th.

### Week 2 (Sept 11–17): Run one or two earning events
Pick **one or two** that your group can actually staff — don't spread thin:
- **Braai / food sale** after church or at month-mid pay period — seswaa, phaphatha, magwinya. Low cost, reliable earner.
- **Car wash** Saturday (P30–P50 per car, get a business to donate water/soap in exchange for a banner).
- **Raffle:** get 3–5 prizes donated by the businesses you wrote to (airtime, grocery voucher, a braai pack), sell tickets at P10–P20 all week, draw publicly on the last day.
- **Pre-independence social evening:** small entrance fee, music, sell food and drinks — doubles as trip promotion.
- Keep chasing sponsorship letters with polite follow-up visits/calls — a letter without a follow-up rarely converts.

### Week 3 (Sept 18–26): Close the gap
- Publish the running total: *"We're at P__ of P__ — help us close the last P__!"* Urgency in the final week converts fence-sitters.
- Second pledge round targeting people who said "remind me at month-end" — **government and many private salaries land around the 20th–25th**, so time your final push for those dates.
- Collect all outstanding pledges; convert any shortfall decisions early (cheaper lodging, shared transport) rather than at the last minute.
- **Sept 26–28:** pay deposits for transport and lodging, confirm bookings in writing.

### Sept 29–30: Travel and celebrate 🇧🇼
- Treasurer publishes the final income/expense statement to the whole group within a week after the trip — this builds the trust that makes **next year's** fundraising easier.

## Step 4 — The PULA / Rain Objects merch drop (our biggest earner)

The rain-motif apparel line (Rain Tee, Let There Be Rain, After Rain crop, beanies, bucket hats, Pula Rain Hoodie) is the strongest asset we have — but with 3 weeks, it only works run one way:

### Run it as a PRE-ORDER, not a stock business
1. **Sell first, print second.** Take paid pre-orders (mobile money upfront, no "pay on delivery") for ~10 days, then do **one production run** sized exactly to orders. Zero stock risk, and the money arrives before the costs do.
2. **Cut the range for this drop.** Launch **2 SKUs max** — the Rain Tee and one headwear piece (bucket hat or beanie). Fewer sizes, fewer decisions, faster printing. Hold the hoodie as a **limited "60 Years" edition of 20–30 units at a premium price** — scarcity sells, and hoodies are the costliest blanks.
3. **Simplify the print for the fundraiser run.** Raised cord/puff detail and reflective ink are beautiful but slow and pricey at local printers. Offer a **standard-print fundraiser edition** now; save the full puff/reflective execution for a proper drop after Independence. Get quotes from 2–3 Gaborone screen printers **this week** — production must be booked by ~Sept 17 to deliver before the 30th.
4. **Price for margin.** Target 45–60% margin. Roughly: tee blank + print ≈ P80–120 → sell P200–250; bucket hat/beanie ≈ P50–80 → sell P150–180; limited hoodie ≈ P200–280 → sell P450–550. 50 tees + 30 hats + 20 hoodies at those margins ≈ **P8,000–12,000 profit**.
5. **The "60 Years" angle is the marketing.** Number the limited pieces (01/60 … 60/60). "Rain made tactile, blessing made visible" + Boipuso 60 is a story people share on WhatsApp statuses for you.

### Where the orders come from
- **Corporate bulk orders:** pitch businesses on tees for their staff to wear on Independence Friday — one order of 15–30 units beats a week of individual sales. This replaces or stacks with the sponsorship letter ask.
- **Group members' networks:** each member is a sales agent with a personal order target (e.g. 5 tees each).
- **Diaspora:** Batswana abroad love home merch for Independence Day — take orders via a trusted contact with PayPal/Wise; ship in one consolidated batch or deliver to their families locally.
- **Order form:** a simple Google Form + mobile money reference number is enough; the treasurer reconciles payments against form entries daily.

### Pre-order timeline
| Dates | Action |
|---|---|
| Sept 4–8 | Printer quotes, final artwork for 2 SKUs, set prices, open order form |
| Sept 8–17 | Selling window (push hard around the WhatsApp launch + payday build-up) |
| Sept 17 | Orders close, confirm final quantities, pay printer deposit |
| Sept 18–24 | Production |
| Sept 25–29 | Distribution — wear it on the 30th 🇧🇼 |

## Step 5 — More vigorous avenues (stack these on top)

- **Sponsorship tiers with merch as the benefit:** P500 = logo shout-out; P1,500 = logo on the event banner + 5 tees; P3,000+ = "official transport/lodging partner" named on everything. Businesses buy visibility, not charity.
- **Ticketed pre-independence social:** entrance fee + food/drink sales + the raffle draw + a merch table in one evening — one event, four income lines.
- **Raffle with donated prizes** sold alongside every tee (P10–P20/ticket, draw at the social).
- **Auction the hoodie #01/60** at the social evening — a single numbered piece can go for several times retail in the room's excitement.

## Step 6 — Sponsorship letter template

> **[Group name / The Skill Seeker Lab]**
> [Contact person, phone, email]
> [Date]
>
> Dear [Business name / Owner],
>
> **Re: Sponsorship request — Botswana's 60th Independence Anniversary trip**
>
> We are [who you are — e.g. a community learning group of __ members from __]. This year Botswana celebrates 60 years of independence, and our group is travelling to [destination] to take part in the celebrations on 30 September.
>
> We are raising **P[target]** to cover transportation and lodging, and we are asking [Business name] to support us with a contribution of **P[specific amount]** or in-kind support such as [transport / accommodation / prizes for our raffle].
>
> In return, we will [display your banner at our events, acknowledge you on our social media and at the celebration, feature your logo on our trip T-shirts].
>
> Contributions can be made via [payment details]. We are happy to visit you to discuss. Thank you for supporting your community.
>
> Pula!
> [Name, role, signature]

**Tip:** always name a specific amount and a specific benefit. "Any support welcome" gets less than "P500 puts your logo on 25 T-shirts."

## Step 7 — Tracking

Keep one shared sheet (paper or Google Sheets) with three tabs:

1. **Pledges:** name, amount promised, amount received, date.
2. **Sponsors:** business, contact, letter sent, follow-up date, outcome.
3. **Events:** event, costs, takings, profit.

---

## Quick reality check

In 3 weeks, the money almost always comes (in order of reliability):
1. **Member pledges from personal networks** — fastest, start today.
2. **Merch pre-orders** — high margin and zero risk *if* run sell-first/print-second with the window closing by Sept 17.
3. **One well-run social event** — food, raffle, merch table, and the hoodie auction in one evening.
4. **Local business sponsorship / bulk tee orders** — slower, but the 60-years angle helps; in-kind (a bus, rooms) is the biggest single win available.
5. Online/diaspora — a bonus, not the plan.

Start the pledge drive **today**, get printer quotes and letters out **this week**, and put your event on the **payday weekend**.
