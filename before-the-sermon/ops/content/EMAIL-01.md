# Email — Kit setup and the first three emails
*Tool decided 25 Sep (D-06): **Kit** (kit.com), free plan — 10,000 subscribers, unlimited emails, one form, one landing page, no automations. MailerLite's free tier fell to 250 subscribers in July 2026, which a challenge would outgrow in weeks. Kit's form "incentive email" delivers a PDF on confirmation without an automation, which is all the Sunday Page needs. The repeat-purchase emails are manual broadcasts to a tag until a paid plan is justified.*

## Setup — Gao, ~10 minutes, her account
1. kit.com → **Sign up** → free plan. Account/brand name: `Before the Sermon`. Sender: gao@dustoffreset.com (or a beforesermon address once one exists). Skip the onboarding tour.
2. **Grow → Landing Pages & Forms → Create new → Form → Inline → "Clean"** (or any minimal template). Name it `Sunday Page`.
3. Form copy below. Settings → **Incentive email: ON** → upload `the-sunday-page_letter.pdf` as the download → button text `Send me the page` → paste the incentive email below.
4. Settings → after subscribing: show the message *"Check your inbox — the page is on its way."*
5. Copy the form's **share link**. That link goes in the Instagram bio and on the website. Send it to Claude.
6. **Settings → Email → footer:** address is required by law (a PO box or a virtual address is fine — never a home address).
7. Create two **tags**: `sunday-page` (applied by the form automatically: Form → Settings → Tag) and `bought-notebook` (applied by hand when someone tells us they bought).

## Form copy
**Heading:** The Sunday Page
**Body:** One printable page. Six questions to sit with any passage before anyone explains it. Free, and yours to print as often as you like.
**Field:** Email address
**Button:** Send me the page
**Under the button (small):** One email most Sundays. Leave in one click.

## Sunday email 01 — send the first Sunday after the notebook is live
**Subject:** Before Sunday: Psalm 1
> Read Psalm 1 tonight. Twice — once fast, once slow.
>
> Then one question: *what keeps pulling your attention?* A word, a phrase, a turn you can't get past. Write it down, even if it's one word. You don't have to know why yet.
>
> Tomorrow, if you hear it preached, notice where the preacher goes somewhere you didn't — and where you went somewhere they didn't. That gap is where you start to become a reader.
>
> The notebook has fifty-two of these. [It's on Amazon now.]
>
> — Before the Sermon
