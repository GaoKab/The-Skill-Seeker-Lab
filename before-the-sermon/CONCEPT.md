## Positioning

> A guided reading companion designed to help you engage Scripture slowly,
> thoughtfully, and without intimidation — before explanation, interpretation,
> or performance.

> Before the Sermon: Sitting With Scripture is a guided reading notebook designed
> to help people engage Scripture slowly, thoughtfully, and without intimidation.
> It is not a devotional, commentary, or theology guide. It does not explain
> passages or tell readers what to believe.
>
> Instead, it offers a structured way to sit with the text, notice what is
> written, reflect honestly, and build confidence as a reader — before sermons,
> summaries, or conclusions. The notebook is meant to be used alongside the
> Bible, not instead of it.

## Who it's for
- People who feel intimidated by the Bible
- Readers who don't know how to read Scripture on their own
- Christians who want to slow down their reading
- Seekers or non-Christians who are curious but cautious
- Anyone who wants to engage the Bible without pressure, performance, or expertise

> No prior knowledge, belief, or background is assumed.

## How it works
> The notebook consists primarily of repeating guided spreads designed to be used
> during Scripture reading sessions.
> The same set of questions repeats throughout to build familiarity and confidence.

### The six prompts — TWO VERSIONS EXIST, THESE CONFLICT

**A. From the interior mockup** (second person, what the reader answers).
Header: `Passage: ______   Date: ______`
1. What did you observe?
2. What stands out or keeps pulling your attention?
3. What confused you or felt unclear?
4. What questions arose — even uncomfortable ones?
5. What did you feel as you read?
6. What do you think this text is exploring?
*Optional: What might this text be asking of you?*

**B. From the website** ("Each Spread Prompts You To:", imperative).
1. Observe what the text actually says
2. Notice repetition, emphasis, and voice
3. Identify assumptions you may be bringing
4. Acknowledge confusion without rushing to resolve it
5. Reflect on how the passage touches your life
6. Choose what you want to sit with longer

Note: B has **"Identify assumptions you may be bringing"**, which A lacks.
A has **"What did you feel as you read?"**, which B lacks.
A's #1 and #2 overlap ("observe" vs "stands out").

### The three movements (site, expanded copy)
- **Observe** — Notice what the text actually says: repetition, emphasis, and voice, without rushing to conclusions.
- **Acknowledge** — Acknowledge confusion or discomfort. Sit with questions. There are no right answers required.
- **Decide** — Decide what you want to sit with longer. Let meaning surface slowly rather than being imposed.

## What makes it different

**This notebook does NOT:**
- Interpret Scripture for you
- Push application or action
- Assume belief or expertise

**Its purpose is to:**
- Change how you read
- Train attention and patience
- Allow meaning to surface slowly
- Build honest reading confidence

> Confusion, silence, and "I don't know" are treated as valid outcomes.

## The voice of this notebook
| Is | Is not |
|---|---|
| Calm | not authoritative |
| Invitational | not instructional |
| Reflective | not prescriptive |
| Gentle | but serious |

> It avoids religious jargon, moralizing language, and pressure to apply or conclude.
